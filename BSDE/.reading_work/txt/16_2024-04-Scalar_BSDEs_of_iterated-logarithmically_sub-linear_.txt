We establish a general existence and uniqueness result of integrable adapted solutions to scalar backward stochastic differential equations with integrable parameters, where the generator 𝑔 has an iteratedlogarithmically uniform continuity in the second unknown variable 𝑧. The result improves our previous one
in Fan et al. (2023), and can be applied to the (conditional) 𝑔-expectation and the star-shaped risk measure.

Nonlinear BSDEs were initially introduced in [1], which gave an existence and uniqueness result on adapted solutions of multidimensional
BSDEs with uniformly Lipschitz continuous generators and square integrable parameters. Since then, BSDEs have been extensively studied due
to their deep connections with various fields such as partial differential

equations, mathematical finance, stochastic control and so on. The
reader is referred to among others [2–4] for more details. In particular,
numerous efforts have been devoted to the well-posedness of adapted
solutions to BSDEs under various integrabilities on the parameters
and various growths and/or continuities of the generator 𝑔 in the
unknown variables (𝑦, 𝑧). For instance, some classical results can be
found in [3,5–14] and the references therein.
With adapted solutions of BSDEs, Peng [15] introduced the notion
of conditional 𝑔-expectation of a square-integrable random variable,
which is a nonlinear extension of the conventional conditional expectation. Since the conventional conditional expectation is defined in the
space of integrable random variables, it is then asked that how to define
the conditional 𝑔-expectation of an only integrable random variable—
which entails solution of BSDEs with only integrable parameters. It has
been widely recognized that it is more difficult to solve BSDEs with
only integrable parameters than those with 𝐿𝑝 -integrable parameters
(𝑝 > 1). To the best of our knowledge, there are only few discussions on
adapted solution of BSDEs with integrable parameters. In particular, an
existence and uniqueness result is available in [16] in this direction for
multidimensional BSDEs, where the generator 𝑔 is Lipschitz continuous
and grows in 𝑧 in a sub-linear way (see for example (H2S)𝛼 with 𝛼 ∈
(0, 1) in Section 2). Subsequently in [17–20], new types of growths and
continuities of the generator 𝑔 in 𝑧 are identified for the well-posedness
of the integrable adapted solutions, such as the uniform continuity

The present paper aims at studying integrable solutions of scalar
BSDEs with only integrable parameters under finer assumptions on the
generator 𝑔, as a continuation of our previous work [21]. The main
result is formulated in Theorem 2.2 in Section 2. It gives the existence
of an 𝐿1 solution of a BSDE with integrable parameters when the
generator 𝑔 has an iterated-logarithmically sub-linear growth in 𝑧 (see
(H2)𝜆𝑛 with 𝑛 ≥ 2 and 𝜆 > 1∕2 in Section 2), and the uniqueness of
integrable solutions in a proper space when the generator 𝑔 further
has an iterated-logarithmically uniform continuity in 𝑧 (see (H5)𝜆𝑛 with
𝑛 ≥ 2 and 𝜆 > 1∕2 in Section 2), which improves that of [21] (see
(i) of Remark 2.3 in Section 2 for details). In fact, it gives a sequence
of weaker and weaker conditions on the generator 𝑔 for the existence
and uniqueness of the integrable solution of BSDE(𝜉, 𝑔) under only
integrable parameters. For the existence, we first establish a crucial
inequality (see Proposition 3.2 in Section 3), and then find a proper
test function (see (3.23) before Proposition 3.4 in Section 3) to apply
Itô’s formula to obtain an a priori bound on the first component of
adapted solutions to the approximating BSDEs (see Proposition 3.5 in
Section 3), and finally utilize the localization technique to obtain the
desired solution. For the uniqueness, we establish a general comparison
theorem for the integrable solutions to the BSDEs (see Proposition 2.4
in Section 2), where the same a priori estimate technique as above and
Theorem 2.1 of [18] play a key role.
In comparison to our previous work [21], the present paper still
appeals to the methodology of test functions, and the key, also the
main difficulty, is to find out a proper test function for the iteratedlogarithmically sub-linear generator such that the inequality (3.24) is
satisfied, which is carried out in Proposition 3.4 through establishing
two elementary inequalities in Propositions 3.1 and 3.2. Furthermore,
we mention applications of our Theorem 2.2 to the (conditional) 𝑔expectation and to the star-shaped risk measure at the end of Section 2.
The consequence of our Theorem 2.2 to the Feynman–Kac formula for
solutions of semi-linear parabolic equations, as one referee asks in the
first round of review, would be very interesting, but it does not seem to
be straightforward and remains, together with the relevant stability of
solutions to BSDEs with iterated-logarithmically sub-linear generators,
to be discussed later.
