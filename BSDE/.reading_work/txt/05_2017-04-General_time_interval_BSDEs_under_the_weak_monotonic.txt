ABSTRACT

ARTICLE HISTORY

The main purpose of this paper is to prove an existence and
uniqueness result for solutions of a multidimensional backward
stochastic diﬀerential equation (BSDE) with a general time interval
(including the deterministic and stochastic cases), where the
generator g of the BSDE is weakly monotonic and of general
growth in y, and Lipschitz continuous in z, both non-uniformly with
respect to t. And, the corresponding comparison theorem for the
solutions of one-dimensional BSDEs is provided. As applications,
we establish a nonlinear Doob-Meyer’s decomposition theorem
for general continuous g-supermartingales under an additional
assumption of the generator g. Some new problems in our setting
arise naturally and are well overcome. These results generalize and
improve some known works.

result of the square integrable solutions when the generator g is uniformly Lipschitz
continuous in both y and z. Since then, there has been a tremendous interest in relaxing
conditions of BSDEs from diﬀerent aspects in order to apply the corresponding existence
and uniqueness results to other ﬁelds, such as weakening the Lipschitz condition on g
(see [3,11,13,14,18] and references therein), improving the ﬁnite terminal time into the
general case (see [5,10,19], etc.), and studying the solutions under non-square integrable
parameters (see [2,8], etc.).
To our knowledge, El Karoui et al. [7] ﬁrst proposed the notation of g-supersolution by
virtue of BSDEs, where the generator g satisﬁes the uniformly Lipschitz condition in (y, z)
and V is an increasing, (Ft )-adapted and RCLL process with V0 = 0. A g-supersolution
of a linear BSDE is actually a superhedging strategy. And g-supersolutions facilitate the
study of reﬂected BSDEs, see Peng and Xu [23] and Klimsiak [12]. The former concluded
that solving a reﬂected BSDE is equivalent to ﬁnding the smallest g-supersolution of the
BSDE with a special constraint. In this spirit, Peng and Xu [24] also solved an American
option pricing problem in an incomplete market. Furthermore, Peng [21] provided the
notion of g-martingales and proved a nonlinear Doob-Meyer’s decomposition theorem for
g-supermartingales by constructing a monotonic limit theorem for g-supersolutions. On
the one hand, g-martingale is a generalization of classical martingales, i.e. g-martingales
become linear martingales when g = 0; on the other hand, g-(super)martingales have close
connection with g-expectations/evaluations, ﬁltration-consistent nonlinear expectations
and dynamic risk measures. We refer the readers to Bayraktar and Yao [1], Coquet et al.
[6], Ma and Yao [17] and Peng [22] for more detailed relationships among them, as well
as some basic properties of g-(super)martingales.
Lin [15,16] considered respectively the nonlinear Doob-Meyer’s decomposition of
g-supermartingales with respect to a general ﬁltration and with jumps. Ma and Yao
[17] researched the nonlinear decomposition, optional sampling theorem and upcrossing
inequality for g-supermartingales under the quadratic growth condition. Shi et al. [25]
studied the nonlinear decomposition of continuous g-supermartingales with uniformly
continuous generators. For the purpose of solving reﬂected BSDEs, Klimsiak [12] solved
BSDE (1) of one-dimensional case where the condition required by g is monotonicity
and general growth in y, and Lipschitz continuity in z. Recently, Fan and Jiang [9]
investigated the existence and uniqueness for solutions of multidimensional BSDEs where
the condition for g with respect to y is extended to a weak monotonicity condition. This
condition uniﬁes some existing classical conditions, such as the monotonicity condition,
Mao’s condition, Osgood’s condition and Constantin’s condition. Additionally, motivated
by the classical martingales theory, Chen and Wang [5] improved the result of Pardoux
and Peng [20] to the general time interval case and obtained the convergence theorem of
g-martingales.
Inspired by above results, we will study BSDE (1) with a general time interval under
the weak monotonicity condition. We ﬁrst show an existence and uniqueness result
for a solution of BSDE (1) with a general time interval (0 ≤ T ≤ ∞), where the
generator g satisﬁes a weak monotonicity condition with a general growth condition
in y, and a Lipschitz continuity condition in z, both non-uniformly with respect to t
(see Theorem 6). We also improve this result to a stochastic time interval case, i.e. the
terminal time is a stopping time valued in [0, T] (see Theorem 12). And then we prove
the corresponding comparison theorem for the solutions of one-dimensional BSDEs (see

Here, we would like to emphasize the following three things. The ﬁrst one is that when
proving the existence result, Pardoux [19] and Fan and Jiang [9] ﬁrst truncated g(·, 0, 0)
and made a convolution to obtain the local Lipschitz condition, and then truncated the
variable y to attain the global Lipschitz condition. Briand et al. [2] used a similar method
to deal with the more general growth condition. Yet we think that truncating directly
g(·, y, z) in the whole domain is more preferable in order to dominate the growth of
g and its gradient. Surprisingly, due to this change we drop some intermediate steps
and simplify greatly the proof procedure (see the ﬁrst and second steps in the proof of
Proposition 10 for details). The second thing needed to mention is that the existence and
uniqueness result of the stochastic time interval case is not trivial. It is because the right
hand side of the weak monotonicity inequality is a nonlinear function, which leads to
the invalidation of methods used by Pardoux [19] and others. Here the stochastic time
interval case is treated by the aid of the general time interval case. The third things should
be stressed is that in the proof of nonlinear Doob-Meyer’s decomposition theorem for
general g-supermartingales, some new diﬃculties arise naturally due to the general time
intervals and the general growth condition required by g in y. First of all, those techniques
used in Shi et al. [25] (especially the technique in their Proposition 11) fail to obtain
the nonlinear decomposition. Secondly, the weak convergence method used in Peng [21]
can not be adopted directly in our setting since the boundedness of the process sequence
involved the generator part in the usual M2 space (see the deﬁnition below) does not
hold any longer due to the fact that T may be inﬁnite and g may have a general growth
in y, which leads to a loss of the corresponding weak compactness. These diﬃculties
are successfully overcome by studying the weak sequential compactness of the above
sequence in a new process space and employing the stopping time technique to treat the
general growth condition of g in y (see the third step in the proof of Theorem 18 for
details).
