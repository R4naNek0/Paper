Abstract
This paper is devoted to solving one-dimensional backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs),
where the time horizon may be finite or infinite and the assumptions on the generator g are not necessary
to be uniform on t. We first show the existence of the minimal solution for this kind of BSDEs with linear
growth generators. Then, we establish a general comparison theorem for solutions of this kind of BSDEs
with weakly monotonic and uniformly continuous generators. Finally, we give an existence and uniqueness
result for solutions of this kind of BSDEs with uniformly continuous generators.

where T > 0 is a finite or an infinite constant called the time horizon, ξ is a square integral
random variable called the terminal condition, the random function g(ω, t, y, z) : Ω × [0, T ] ×
R × Rd → R is progressively measurable for each (y, z), called the generator of the BSDE (1),
and B is a d-dimensional Brownian motion. The solution (y· , z · ) is a pair of square integrable,
adapted processes. The triple (ξ, T, g) is called the parameters of the BSDE (1).
Such equations, in the nonlinear case, were first introduced by Pardoux and Peng [16], who
established an existence and uniqueness result for solutions to multidimensional BSDEs under
the Lipschitz assumption of the generator g. Since then, BSDEs have been studied with great
interest, and they have gradually become an important mathematical tool in many fields such as
financial mathematics, stochastic games and optimal control, partial differential equations, etc.
Even though there exist some improvements of the Pardoux–Peng result for multidimensional
BSDEs (see e.g. [15,17,8,11,1], and in particular, [6] and [7] where the time horizon T may be
infinite and the assumption with respect to the generator g is not necessary to be uniform with
respect to t), the case of real-valued BSDEs meaning that y· is a real process is easier to handle
(see e.g. [13,2,4,3] etc.). In particular, provided that 0 < T < +∞ and the assumptions with
respect to the generator g are uniform with respect to t. Lepeltier and San Martin [14] showed
the existence and uniqueness of the minimal solution for BSDEs when their generators g are
continuous and have a linear growth, and Jia [12] studied the existence and uniqueness of the
solution for BSDEs when their generator g are independent of y and uniformly continuous in
z. The main reason for the gap between the real case and the multidimensional one comes from
the comparison theorem. Roughly speaking, a comparison theorem for BSDEs allows one to
compare solutions to BSDEs by comparing the terminal conditions and the generators. Since the
pioneering work by Peng [18] on the comparison principle for BSDEs (see also Theorem 2.2 in
[9]), three general comparison theorems were respectively obtained by Cao and Yan [5], Chen
and Wang [7] and Briand and Hu [2]. By the way, let us also mention that, in our knowledge, the
monotonicity condition and the uniform continuity condition of the generator g in y were first
put forward in [17,11] respectively.
Motivated by these results, we are interested in solving one-dimensional BSDEs, where the
time horizon T may be finite or infinite and the assumptions with respect to the generator g are
not necessary to be uniform with respect to t. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is
devoted to the existence of the minimal solution for this kind of BSDEs when their generators g
are continuous and have a linear growth (see Theorem 1 in Section 2), which improves the results
in [14]. Furthermore, in Section 3, we establish a general comparison theorem for solutions of this
kind of BSDEs with generators satisfying some kind of weak monotonicity condition in y and
uniform continuity condition in z (see Theorem 2 in Section 3), which can be seen as an extension
for four classical comparison theorems mentioned above, Theorem 2.2 in [9], Theorem 2.1 in
[5], Theorem A.2 in [7] and Proposition 5 in [2]. Finally, in Section 4, by virtue of the above
two results we give an existence and uniqueness result for solutions of this kind of BSDEs
with generators satisfying some kind of strong uniform continuity condition in y and uniform
continuity condition in z (see Theorem 3 in Section 4). This generalizes some corresponding
results in [16,15,7,8,11,12] in the one-dimensional setting.

4. The existence and uniqueness result
As applications of Theorems 1 and 2, we will establish a general uniqueness and existence
theorem (see Theorem 3) for BSDEs in this section, which generalizes the corresponding results
in [16,15,7,8,11,12] in the one-dimensional setting. We need the following assumption on g,
where we also assume that 0 < T ≤ +∞.
(H5) g is strongly uniformly continuous in y and uniformly continuous in z, i.e., dP ×dt −a.s.,
for each y1 , y2 , z 1 , z 2 ,
|g(ω, t, y1 , z 1 ) − g(ω, t, y2 , z 2 )| ≤ u(t)ρ(|y1 − y2 |) + v(t)φ(|z 1 − z 2 |),
where u(·) and ρ(·) are defined in (H3), and v(·) and φ(·) are defined in (H4).
Remark 7. By the classical theory of uniformly continuous functions, the concavity condition
with respect to ρ(·) in (H3′ b), (H3′ b′ ) and (H5) can be taken away. Furthermore, the condition
of linear growth with respect to φ(·) in (H4), (H4′ ) and (H5) can also be eliminated.
The following Theorem 3 is the main result of this section.
Theorem 3. Assume that 0 < T ≤ +∞ and that g satisfies (H5) and (A2) in Section 2. Then
for each ξ ∈ L 2 (Ω , FT , P), the BSDE with parameters (ξ, T, g) has a unique solution.
Proof. The uniqueness result follows from Theorem 2 or Corollary 2 due to the fact that (H5)
implies (H3) and (H4). In what follows, we show the existence part. In fact, it follows from
assumption (H5) that
|g(ω, t, y, z)| ≤ |g(ω, t, y, z) − g(ω, t, 0, 0)| + |g(ω, t, 0, 0)|
≤ u(t)ρ(|y|) + v(t)φ(|z|) + |g(ω, t, 0, 0)|
≤ ku(t)|y| + av(t)|z| + |g(ω, t, 0, 0)| + ku(t) + bv(t)
Thus, in view of (A2) and (H5), we know that g satisfies assumptions (H2) and (H1) with
f t = |g(ω, t, 0, 0)| + ku(t) + bv(t). Thus, the existence result follows from Theorem 1, and
the proof is complete. 
Remark 8. It is clear that Theorem 3 generalizes the corresponding result in [7] (see Lemma 1
in Section 2) by taking ρ(x) = φ(x) = x.
In particular, by letting 0 < T < +∞ and u(t) = v(t) = 1 in assumption (H5) of Theorem 3
we obtain the following corollary.
Corollary 3. Assume that 0 < T < +∞ and g satisfies assumptions (H3′ b), (H4′ ) and (A2).
Then for each ξ ∈ L 2 (Ω , FT , P), the BSDE with parameters (ξ, T, g) has a unique solution.
Remark 9. In view of Remark 6 and Hölder’s inequality, it is easy to see that Theorem 3 and
Corollary 3 are also an improvement for the corresponding (one-dimensional) ones in [15,11,12,
16,8].
