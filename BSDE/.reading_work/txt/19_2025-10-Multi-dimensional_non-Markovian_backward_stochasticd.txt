Abstract
This paper is devoted to a general solvability of multi-dimensional non-Markovian
backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) with interactively quadratic generators. General structures of the generator g are posed for both local and global
existence and uniqueness results on a BSDE, which admit a general growth of the
generator g(ω, t, y, z) in the state variable y , and a quadratic growth of the ith components g i (ω, t, y, z) both in the j th row z j of the state variable z for j 6= i (which we call
the “interactively quadratic ” growth) and in the ith row z i of z . We first establish an
existence and uniqueness result on local bounded solutions and then several existence
and uniqueness results on global bounded and unbounded solutions. They improve
several existing works in the non-Markovian setting, and also incorporate some interesting examples, which include global bounded solution of backward stochastic
Burger differential systems and a partial answer to the problem posed in Jackson [25]
(see Example 2.16(ii) and Corollary 2.25, respectively). A comprehensive study on
the bounded solution of scalar-valued quadratic BSDEs with unbounded stochastic
parameters is carried out for deriving our main results.
Keywords: system of BSDEs; interactively quadratic generator; backward stochastic Burger
differential systems; BMO martingale; bounded solution; unbounded solution.

generator g(ω, t, y, z) : Ω × [0, T ] × Rn × Rn×d → Rn is (Ft )-progressively measurable for

said to be quadratic if the generator g has a quadratic growth in the last argument z .
Bismut [3] initially introduced in 1973 the above BSDE in the linear case, and
further studied in 1976, in his alternative work [4], the backward stochastic Riccati
differential system as a specially structured matrix-valued quadratic nonlinear BSDE.
In 1990, Pardoux and Peng [38] established the existence and uniqueness result for a
multidimensional uniformly Lipschitz continuous nonlinear BSDE. Since then, there has
been an increasing interest in BSDEs and their applications in diverse fields such as
mathematical finance, stochastic control, partial differential equations (PDEs), etc. The
interested reader is referred to, among others, El Karoui et al. [13], Kobylanski [30], El
Karoui and Hamadène [12], Hu et al. [23], Frei and Dos Reis [21], Kramkov and Pulido
[34], Escauriaza et al. [14], Tian [42], and Weston [43].
A quadratic BSDE naturally arises from non-Markovian linear quadratic optimal
stochastic control, risk-sensitive control, stochastic differential games, and stochastic
economic equilibrium (see, in particular, Bismut [4] and El Karoui and Hamadène [12]).
When n = 1 and the terminal value is bounded, the existence and uniqueness result was
first given by Kobylanski [30], and subsequently by Kohlmann and Tang [31], Tevzadze
[41], Briand and Elie [6], Fan [15], and Luo and Fan [36]. When n = 1 and the terminal
value is allowed to be unbounded, existence and uniqueness of unbounded solutions of
quadratic BSDEs was given in Briand and Hu [7, 8], Delbaen et al. [10, 11], Barrieu and
El Karoui [1], and Fan et al. [16]. A system of quadratic BSDEs is widely known to be
more difficult to be solved (see Peng [39]). In particular, the solution of general backward
stochastic Riccati differential systems was already deeply studied by Bismut [4], as early
as in 1976—almost from the birth of a BSDE. It is known as the so-called Bismut’s open
problem, and was partially solved by Kohlmann and Tang [32] and was completely solved
by Tang [40]. A general existence and uniqueness, without any structural assumption
on the quadratic growth of the generator, is recognized to be impossible, as shown by
Frei and Dos Reis [21] with a counterexample. On the other hand, diverse systems
of quadratic BSDEs have been derived from diverse problems such as financial priceimpact modeling, financial market equilibrium among interacting agents, nonzero-sum
risk-sensitive stochastic differential games, and stochastic equilibrium in an incomplete
financial markets. Much progress on them has been made. When the terminal value
is very small in the essential supremum norm, Tevzadze [41] established a general
existence and uniqueness result, which has inspired numerous studies on the bounded
or unbounded solution under some “smallness” of the terminal value, the terminal time,
or the generator; see for example Frei [20], Jamneshan et al. [27], Kramkov and Pulido
[34, 33], Harter and Richou [22] and Kardaras et al. [28]. In the particular Markovian
setting, Cheridito and Nam [9] proved the global solvability of a special system of
quadratic BSDEs associated to a forward SDE, and Xing and Žitković [45] formulated
a large class of systems of quadratic BSDEs via the Bensoussan-Frehse (BF) condition

Numerous works are also available on bounded solutions in the non-Markovian
setting, without smallness nor Malliavin-regularity of the terminal time, the terminal

component g i (t, y, z) of the generator g(t, y, z) admits a quadratic growth only in the
ith row z i of the matrix variable z (i.e. in the so-called “diagonally quadratic ” way)
and is bounded in z j for each j 6= i, and consequently answered the open problem
arising from a nonzero-sum risk-sensitive stochastic differential game posed by El Karoui
and Hamadène [12]. When g i (t, y, z) has a strictly quadratic growth in z i for each
i = 1, . . . , n, Fan et al. [17] further extended [24] to allow the generator g(t, y, z) to have
a sub-quadratic growth in z . Luo [37] solved a system of BSDEs, where g i (t, y, z) grows
quadratically in z j for any 1 ≤ j ≤ i (i.e. in the so-called “triangularly quadratic ” way)
and has a special form. Very recently, Fan et al. [19] generalized [17] to the system
of quadratic backward stochastic Volterra integral equations, and Weston [43] applied
the result of [17] to prove the global existence of a Radner equilibrium in a limited
participation economy model.
The present paper focuses on both local and global solution of systems of nonMarkovian BSDEs with interactively quadratic generators g , that is, the components
g i (ω, t, y, z) of the generators are allowed to have a quadratic growth both in z i and in
z j with j 6= i. Our results unify and extend some existing results in the non-Markovian
setting such as [24], [37] and [17], and show that under some circumstances, a system
of quadratic BSDEs has a√unique solution when the generator g(t, y, z) has a quadratic
growth in the product of θ and some rows of the matrix variable z for a small constant
θ, which is compared to some existing results mentioned in the second last paragraph.

In comparison especially to Hu and Tang [24] and Fan et al. [17], our methods manifest the following several features. First, bounded solution of scalar-valued quadratic
BSDEs with unbounded diversely integrable stochastic parameters, is comprehensively
investigated with the BMO martingale (i.e., bounded mean oscillation martingale) theory,
Girsanov’s transform and some helpful inequalities. New results include existence,
uniqueness and a priori estimates; see Propositions A.1 to A.3 in the Appendix for details.
Second, in the proof of Theorem 2.3, we construct a novel contraction mapping on some
closed convex subset in the space of bounded solutions of systems of quadratic BSDEs,
via bounded solution of scalar-valued BSDEs in contrast to those of [24, 17]. Third,
we use a delicate induction combined with Propositions A.1 to A.3 and Theorem 2.3
in the proof of Theorems 2.10 and 2.14. Finally, in the proof of Theorems 2.26, 2.27
and 2.31, we transfer the BSDE with an unbounded BMO terminal value into one with
a bounded terminal value and a generator satisfying the assumptions of Theorems 2.6,
2.10 and 2.14. We note that for the unbounded global solution of a system of quadratic
BSDEs, in [17, Theorem 2.8], the terminal value ξ can have only exponential moments
of arbitrary order, while the generator g is forced to satisfy some stronger growth and
continuity than Assumptions (D1) and (D2).
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce notations,
state the main results, and present interesting examples, some of them arising from
practical applications, along with remarks to illustrate the novelty of our results. The
proof is given if it is not lengthy, and only outlined otherwise. In Section 3, we prove the
local bounded solutions (Theorem 2.3), and in Section 4, we prove the global bounded
solutions (Theorems 2.10 and 2.14). The global unbounded solution (Theorem 2.27)
is proved finally in Section 5. In the Appendix, we give as auxiliary results bounded
solutions of scalar-valued quadratic BSDEs with unbounded stochastic parameters.
